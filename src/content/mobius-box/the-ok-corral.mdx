We needed one fight to measure combat by, and it had to be a real one. The
gunfight at the O.K. Corral is nine men on a narrow lot beside Fly's
boarding house, built from Western Hero archetypes and carrying real
weapon builds. Every Phase, each man picks from the engine's menu of legal
actions. The fight tells you two things at once: how long it lasts, and
what everyone chose to do. A short fight, or one action chosen over and
over, means something's wrong.

The first run was over in 7 Phases, and in the early runs 11 of every 13
actions taken were Multiple Attacks. That turned out to be the right call under the engine as
it was, because the engine had Multiple Attack wrong. 6E2 p.73 charges the
same penalty on every roll in the sequence, and a miss on any one of them
means the rest miss too. Kirby was charging a shrinking penalty instead,
and letting the sequence carry on after a miss. So the first shot cost the
same as a single attack, and every shot after it was free. Nobody should
ever shoot once under those rules, and nobody did.

The second defect was in the guns. The Colt Peacemaker's damage comes from
a level of Killing Attack plus an adder that moves it up a rung, and that
adder was being skipped. Five of the nine men carried a Peacemaker, and
they'd all been firing most of a die short.

Over that day's fixes the fights went from 7 Phases to 13, then 24, then
41, and the choices spread out between single shots, Multiple Attacks and moving to
strike.

Then I asked the obvious question: why is nobody diving for cover? There
were three reasons, stacked. The engine worked out cover correctly, and the
only thing that read the result was the description of the scene. Taking
cover changed nobody's chance to be hit, so it was worth nothing. Second,
it scored each piece of cover against the worst angle from any enemy. With
nine men on a lot that narrow everybody is flanked by somebody, so every
piece scored zero. And third, the lot had nothing to hide behind: both
buildings run parallel to the firing line, beside the shooters rather than
between them. We added barrels, a trough and some crates, and fixed the
other two. The first turn of the next fight had 8 men taking cover.

The lesson from the third reason is the one I'd keep. For a while the
honest answer to "why no cover?" was "the map has none." That was true, and
it hid the first two defects completely. When the map explains it, check
the engine anyway.

One thing the benchmark can't fix: the Cowboys win every run. Their
archetypes are simply better built than the Earps', and historically the
Earps won. We did fix one thing on the history side: Ike Clanton and Billy Claiborne
were unarmed that day, and in the first version they weren't.
