Anyone who has played a Multipower character knows the moment. You've got
60 points in the reserve, the villain is 30 metres off and closing, and you
want some of your Blast and some of your Flight. A variable slot lets you do
exactly that (6E1 p.404&ndash;406): put 40 points into the Blast and 20
into Flight, and you've got an 8d6 Blast and 20 metres of Flight this Phase.

Until this week Kirby couldn't do it. The arithmetic for a split had been in
the engine for months, and nothing ever called it. Every slot was either on
at full strength or off. So a Multipower worked like a light switch, not a
dial, which is the whole point of the variable slot gone.

Now it's a dial. A split is points per slot, written to the fight's log like
any other action. [kirby-cost](/docs/kirby-cost/) works out what those
points buy &mdash; how many dice, how many metres, how much PD and ED &mdash;
the same way Hero Designer would price the slot. Advantages count, so 30
points in a Flight bought with Reduced Endurance buys 24 metres, not 30.
The book's own Kerendor example runs against the engine split by split, and
every number agrees. It's on the [RAW alignment](/docs/raw-alignment/) page.

Two things came out of how we actually play. Every Multipower states its
opening split before Segment 12, because that's how my table has always
done it &mdash; nobody starts a fight with the reserve in a heap. And a
character can carry named splits, including a standard combat set, because
I know players who have one and reach for it every fight. The engine offers
those first.

The part worth writing down is what the final review caught. The plan
covered attacks, movement and defenses, and the tests for all three passed.
But Drain, Aid, Flash, Entangle, Mind Control and Mind Scan don't read
their dice the way an attack does, and none of them looked at the split. A
slot with zero points in it would still fire at full strength. And
characteristic slots &mdash; a Multipower slot of extra STR, say &mdash;
were offered in the menu and then did nothing at all. Both are fixed, both
before release, and both are the same lesson as last time: the tests were
written against the plan, and the plan was incomplete.

Not everything is a dial yet. A few powers are priced flat rather than per
level &mdash; Images, Invisibility, Life Support and the like &mdash; and
for now those slots are either off or full. Darkness also doesn't shrink
when you cut its points, because the engine doesn't size a Darkness field
from its levels yet. Both are written down rather than guessed at.

What's next is the table side: a panel where you set the split yourself,
with the option to ask for a suggestion if you want one. Experienced players
won't want the suggestion first, so it isn't.
