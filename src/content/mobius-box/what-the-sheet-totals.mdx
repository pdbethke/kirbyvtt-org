This one shipped back in September, and it's the biggest single change the
combat engine has had, so it's worth writing up even late.

A HERO character's PD isn't always one number in one place. A brick might
have PD 15 in his Characteristics and another +20 PD bought as a power. On
his character sheet, the one Hero Designer prints, his PD is 35. The combat
engine was reading the Characteristics section, so it fought him at 15.

Once we saw it, we measured it properly. Every character in my library, 794
of them, built two ways and compared. 469 came out identical. 325 fought
differently, and every one of the 325 had a reason we could name. 319 had
bought a characteristic as a power that Hero Designer counts toward the
total. The other 6 had purchases that only apply in their heroic identity,
and combat had ignored those outright &mdash; one hero with DEX 25 had been
fighting as his civilian self at DEX 10. The plan going in predicted
nineteen.

The other direction mattered just as much. A villain with a tail has +20
STR bought with an "Only With Tail" limitation, and Hero Designer marks
that purchase as not counting toward his totals. If we'd simply added
everything up, he'd have punched at 16d6 instead of 12d6 off a STR he
doesn't really have. The rule I settled on: a limitation is not a limitation
if it doesn't limit. Hero Designer already records which purchases count,
so the engine reads that instead of guessing.

Under the hood, a characteristic now has two values. There's the **base**,
which is what the sheet says and is never written to. And there's what's
true right now: the base plus whatever is currently being applied, such as
a Drain, an Aid, or a power that only works some of the time. Keeping the
base untouched is what lets a character go out to a <code>.hdc</code> file and
come back unchanged.

The short version is the one we hold everything to now: combat fights the
character on the sheet.
