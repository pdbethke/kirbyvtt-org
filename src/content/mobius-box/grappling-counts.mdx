I grapple, so I care about this one more than is probably reasonable.

Until September, a Grab in Kirby changed nothing about either fighter's
combat ability. You could grab a man and nothing about the fight was
different. Grappling was free, and it was never worth doing to anyone you
could shoot instead.

Now it costs what it should. Both people in a grapple are at half DCV; the
grabber is at half OCV against anyone else, and the man being held is at
-3 OCV against the man holding him (Western Hero p.104 lays this out
cleanly). Getting free is a dice contest: each side rolls a d6 per 5 STR and
counts BODY, and the grabber wins ties (6E2 p.66). It used to be a straight
comparison, so STR 20 always beat STR 10. Two old tests asserted exactly
that, and they now assert the roll. The held man also gets an immediate
attempt with Casual STR, against the grabber's full STR. And how well he
breaks out matters: still held, free but out of time, or free with his
Phase still to use.

Once you're holding someone, you can throw them.

The best part came from a question I asked while we were testing: can the
man being held catch a bullet that was meant for the man holding him? He
can. 6E2 p.45 covers firing into a melee, and it's built now. Miss the man
you were aiming at by a little, and the shot is rolled again against the
other body in the clinch. It's an optional rule, off unless the GM turns it
on.

Not done yet: a plain Squeeze and Slam (a martial artist's Crush is in, the
basic versions aren't), and a clean way to just let go of someone. Right now
the only way to let go is to throw him.
